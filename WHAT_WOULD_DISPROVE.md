\# TRACEBIND-Albatross: What Would Disprove the Hypothesis



Scientific credibility requires actively seeking falsification. The TRACEBIND-Albatross hypothesis will be considered \*\*unsupported\*\* and the project will pivot or halt if any of the following occur:



1\. \*\*Instability\*\*: Descriptor fields exhibit extreme, unphysical volatility between adjacent atmospheric pressure levels (failing H1).

2\. \*\*Reproducibility Failure\*\*: The descriptor outputs cannot be independently reproduced using the frozen algorithm and the same ERA5 inputs.

3\. \*\*Null Model Failure\*\*: Descriptor outputs for real atmospheric data are statistically indistinguishable from Fourier-phase-scrambled or spatially shuffled null models.

4\. \*\*No Physical Correlation\*\*: Descriptor fields show no statistically significant relationship to independent atmospheric state variables relevant to flight (e.g., vertical velocity, wind shear, Richardson number) after controlling for trivial confounders (failing H2).

5\. \*\*Routing Null Result\*\*: Simplified routing simulations incorporating TRACEBIND descriptors show no measurable improvement in simulated energy expenditure compared to baseline weather-aware routing (failing H4/H5).



\*Note:\* Discovering \*why\* it fails (e.g., "only works in specific mid-latitude jet regimes") is a valid and valuable scientific outcome (Outcome C), but it still disqualifies the broad hypothesis of universal routing utility.
